Offer Construction

How the water value becomes NEMS offers for energy, regulation and reserves: the market's offer rules, the timing of a decision, each step from the SDP's response to a compliant 5-step offer, what the curves look like in practice, and what is still missing.

§2 One decision, in time

  1. A new forecast is issued with data strictly before the decision time.
  2. The SDP is solved for that issue: value function per SoC and price state (11 bins, or 21 nodes in the continuous version), with reserves chosen inside each stage.
  3. The SoC expected at the start of t is computed from the actual SoC and the pending offers cleared under the forecast scenarios.
  4. The reserve mix for t is chosen (§5), then the energy curve beside it (§3–4).
  5. The offer is made compliant and submitted. Earlier offers can't change; later intervals are re-bid at their own gate closure.

§3 From value function to curve

With Markov prices, the value of energy left after t depends on the price at t itself, \(C_t(s' \mid \lambda)\). The curve is the optimal response on a fine price grid (50 points between consecutive price states, plus points beyond the extremes):

\[ q_t(\lambda) = \arg\max_{s' \text{ reachable from } s}\ \Big\{ \lambda\,\big(d - c\big)\Delta \;-\; \kappa\,(c + d)\,\Delta \;+\; C_t(s' \mid \lambda) \Big\}, \qquad \text{MW} = d - c. \]

\(C_t\) already contains the best re-planned future from every \(s'\), so sweeping \(\lambda\) here is equivalent to re-solving the whole future with a different price at t. When \(C_t\) doesn't depend on \(\lambda\) and is concave, the switch points are the familiar water-value thresholds:

\[ \text{discharge if } \lambda > \frac{\mu_{t+1}(s)}{\eta_d} + \kappa, \qquad \text{charge if } \lambda < \eta_c\, \mu_{t+1}(s) - \kappa, \qquad \mu_{t+1} = \frac{\partial C_t}{\partial s}. \]

The response can be non-monotone (a high price announcing higher prices makes charging attractive). One offer must rise with price, so the response is replaced by its closest rising fit (isotonic regression, rising).

§5 Reserve offers

  • Chosen at gate closure. Among the mixes that are best for some price state, take the one with the highest expected value over the price states once the energy action must fit beside it (_choose_reserves). With reserve price states, the mix is valued at its expectation over the reserve state.
  • Offered at 0 SGD/MW/h. Always clears, paid the clearing price. The quantity can't react to the interval's actual reserve price (open loop in the reserve price).
  • Energy curve restricted to actions compatible with the chosen reserves (RC383 power groups, RC386 SoC checks).
  • Regulation energy (AGC, 5% up / 5% down of the MW) is settled at USEP and moves the SoC.

Important TODO: priced reserve offers. A rising curve per product, priced at its opportunity cost: energy margin given up + value of the SoC headroom held (water value over the RC386 band) + expected activation cost. It would be built like the energy curve, by sweeping the reserve price. First check how the NEMS co-optimisation treats a storage unit's energy and reserve curves together.

§8 Gaps and next steps

  1. Hackathon parameters: SoC 5–95 MWh; score is net market revenue, so test κ = 0 in the objective.
  2. Priced reserve offers (§5).
  3. Curves against the SoC distribution: choose the curve for t that maximises expected value over the SoC distribution at t (the SDP already gives it), instead of building it at the expected SoC.
  4. Curves for every later period: standing offers and fallbacks if EMA calls the algorithm less often; refine them by rolling the SoC distribution forward with the chosen curves until they stop changing. If offers really are fixed once a day, optimise all curves jointly over trajectories (an LP; Fleten & Kristoffersen).
  5. Two pending intervals: optimise the curves for t and t + 1 jointly.
  6. Offer fields: maximum combined transfer limit and ramp rates (5.2B.2.5–6).
  7. Price impact: realistic reserve caps or a residual supply curve; with impact, the offer curve chooses the clearing price (supply function theory).
  8. Smarter compression if curves ever have more than 5 steps: choose the steps that lose the least expected value under the price distribution.
