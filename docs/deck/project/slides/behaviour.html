What the water value does differently

The uplift is all September–October

In the spike weeks it earns about +2 M of energy over the self-schedule. In 10 of the other 11 months it trails by 20–70 k, mostly on regulation.

It keeps the battery full

SoC ≥ 80 MWh 57% of the time (self-schedule 21%, perfect foresight 25%). Near full the RC386 charge check leaves no room for regulation: 17–23 MW held vs 30.

Regulation is the lever left

About −2.3 M of regulation vs the self-schedule, +1.3 M of primary and contingency. Likely the firm-reserve rule: its reserves must fit beside any energy that may clear; the self-schedule’s own energy always clears.

Harmless oddities

Half the engine cuts (9 GWh) are small charge top-ups at full SoC: the expected SoC is a mean (99 MWh) when the battery stayed at 100. On spikes the expected SoC can miss by 100 MWh (11 Sep, 13:00).

12 months, half-hourly, NEMS rules. The explorer’s “At gate closure” view shows the forecast and both plans for any interval.

The energy forecast is well calibrated (about 10% of prices above q90, 1% above q99 in most months), so fat tails are not the cause.
